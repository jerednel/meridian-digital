title: "What a 30-day visibility Sprint actually finds"
description: "Five things you should know at the end of thirty days — including whether there is a meaningful opportunity at all."
pubDate: 2026-09-01
author: Jeremy Nelson
---

A Sprint is not a crawl of everything you publish, and it is not a strategy offsite. It is a time-boxed measurement of whether buyers in your category are being pointed at you, at a competitor, or at a source that frames the category without you.

By day 30 you should know five things:

1. **Which buyer questions actually matter.** The Discover–Buy path for your category, not a prompt dump.
2. **Where you appear, and who replaces you.** Presence and omission, question by question, against named competitors.
3. **Which sources form the shortlist.** The pages and third-party mentions the systems used.
4. **The gaps that explain the miss.** On your site, off your site, or both — specific enough to act.
5. **Whether the opportunity is real.** Keep measuring, or stop.

That last item is part of the work. Some companies already occupy the answers that matter. Some categories are not researched this way. Selling a monitor in those cases is theater.

If the gap is real, monitoring is a later decision: the same questions, watched as competitors publish and sources drift, without an annual contract. The Sprint exists so that decision is made with evidence.
