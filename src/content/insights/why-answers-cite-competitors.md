---
title: "Why AI answers cite your competitors"
description: "Answer systems need language they can lift. Scenario pages, roundups, and independent writeups get used. Capabilities pages that never name the job do not."
pubDate: 2026-08-26
author: Jeremy Nelson
---

When a buyer asks how to stop tailgating at a data center, the answer is assembled from pages that already describe that job.

A specialist's scenario guide does this on purpose: facility type, failure mode, alternatives, a named approach. An integrator roundup does it by listing two or three default vendors. A capabilities page that lists modules — access, video, identity — does not. There is nothing to quote when the question is a job, not a product family.

This is why "we have a blog" is not a finding. The relevant test is whether the page can serve as a source for the specific question that precedes a purchase. If it cannot, Google may still index it. AI systems may still crawl it. Neither will use it when composing the shortlist.

The other half of occupancy is off-site. Many answers cite a third party: a roundup, a directory, an analyst-style list, a practitioner writeup. Your own domain is necessary. It is rarely sufficient. A Sprint that only inspects your sitemap will miss the source that actually named the competitor.

The useful output is therefore small: this question, these occupants, these sources, this first move. Re-measure the same question later. Do not expand the backlog to compensate for not wanting to publish the one page the answer needed.
