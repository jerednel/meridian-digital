---
title: "Track questions that can turn into revenue"
description: "Prompt lists are easy to inflate. Discover, Evaluate, Compare, Validate, and Buy are the stages where a name entering or leaving the answer changes a deal."
pubDate: 2026-08-19
author: Jeremy Nelson
---

It is easy to look busy in AI search. Collect hundreds of prompts. Score mention rate. Publish a heatmap. None of that tells a demand leader whether a buyer who is about to spend money was told to consider someone else.

The questions that matter sit on a commercial path:

**Discover.** What options exist for this problem?  
**Evaluate.** What should we check before we buy?  
**Compare.** How does this vendor differ from the others?  
**Validate.** Who uses this in an environment like ours?  
**Buy.** How do we procure this without getting it wrong?

A company can be visible on Discover chatter and absent on Compare. That is a shortlist problem, not a "share of voice" problem. The reverse is also true: a comparison page that never gets asked is decoration.

When we run a Sprint, we refuse to pad the question set. If a prompt cannot plausibly sit on the path to a purchase, it does not belong in the brief. That keeps the output to a handful of findings instead of a catalog of seventy recommendations nobody will ship.

If you already occupy the answers on this path, the honest result is to stop measuring. If you do not, the next question is which sources put the other names there — not how to produce more content in general.
